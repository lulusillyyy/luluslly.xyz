Now in my last post I talked about the Kinesis Advantage keyboard and why I think it's a pretty decent keyboard to use, all things considered. This time around, I'll be doing something similar, except with a different yet similar board. The board in discussion being the Ergodox! And not just *any* Ergodox, but [the low profile version that SliceMK sells!](https://www.slicemk.com/pages/ergodox-wireless) The SliceMK Ergodox Wireless LP (the "LP" stands for "low profile") is a delightful little board that I managed to snag for a pretty penny off the second-hand market.

# All About the Ergodox  

The Ergodox originated in the early 2010's as a DIY kit. It was originally designed by Dominic Beauchamp, but its creation was a result of many different community members working together to create the final product. It borrows from the [Key64 keyboard](https://web.archive.org/web/20190501020156/https://key64.org/) and the Kinesis Model 100, a contoured ergonomic keyboard released 20 years prior in 1992. The Ergodox aims to be a decenteralized keyboard, allowing anyone to create their own keyboard with their own custom firmware if they so please. Not too long after its creation, the [Ergodox EZ by ZSA](https://ergodox-ez.com/) became available in 2015; this gave those looking for a prebuilt board using the same layout a chance to purchase it.

# The Illustrious Layout and its Parallels to the Kinesis Advantage

As previously mentioned, the Ergodox's layout borrows heavily from the Kinesis contoured keyboards, namely the Kinesis Model 100 and later Kinesis Advantage line of ergo boards. The only noticeable differences are (1) the board itself is flat as apposed to the contoured design of the Kinesis Advantages; (2) there are no function keys, but instead an added inner column on both sides with 3 keys per side; and (3) the mechanical switches are hot-swappable. Depending on your preferences, this may be the perfect keyboard for you; it definitely serves as a good introduction into programmable keyboards -- at least in my opinion, these things are very opinionated. 

# Is the Ergodox Better Than the Kinesis?

Personally, if I had to choose between the two, I would go for the Kinesis. My reasoning being that the form factor and subsequent ergonomics make more sense to me -- especially considering that both boards can be priced similarly. If you prefer something more portable and more programmable, I would definitely pick the Ergodox. The Achilles' heel of the Kinesis Advantage 1/2/non-pro 360 is the very limited programming capabilities. Home row modifiers, layer-tap, and the like are simply not possible as compared to any modern custom keyboard like the Dox. In the end, it's really up to you -- the typist -- on what you'd prefer and what you're willing to sacrifice.

# Summary

The Ergodox -- which includes all common variations -- is in my opinion a great beginner ergo board that takes some of the staple features of many of its kind in order to make something that is not only highly customizable in its base form, but is also free to the public to manufacture and modify. Regardless of my own personal gripes with the layout, I would still recommend this to anyone looking to dip their toes in the ergonomic keyboard niche.
